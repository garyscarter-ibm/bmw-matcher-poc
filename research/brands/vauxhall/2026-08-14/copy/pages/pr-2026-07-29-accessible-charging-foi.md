## Headline (EXACT)

> ON-STREET ELECTRIC VEHICLE CHARGING FOR DISABLED DRIVERS DOUBLES IN LAST 12 MONTHS, BUT 75% OF COUNCILS STILL HAVE NO ACCESSIBLE CHARGERS

At 134 characters this is the longest headline on the index. Structure is
**good news comma BUT bad news**: the brand publishes a statistic against the sector, including
against its own interest in a smooth EV narrative. The `, BUT ` pivot is the signature of the
campaign register.

## Summary bullets (fetcher condensed these; exact fragments only)

> - Council provision of accessible on-street charging has `doubled in the last year`
> - Chargers in disabled bays rose `from 105 to 245` since 2024, though `75% of councils still have no accessible chargers`
> - Roughly `15% of on-street chargers` are now adapted for disabled drivers
> - `14% of on-street chargers` conform to the British Standards Institution's PAS 1899:2022 accessible charging standard
> - `[FETCHER PARAPHRASE]` Drivers can register a street and request accessible charging via the Electric Streets of Britain campaign at electricstreets.co.uk

## Spokesperson quote (EXACT sentences)

Attributed to **Eurig Druce**, title as written here: `Managing Director, Vauxhall & Group
Managing Director, Stellantis UK`. **A third variant of the same person's title**, comma-form
this time. Three releases, three formats.

> It's encouraging to see more councils recognising the importance of accessible on-street charging

> shows how much further there is to go.

> As more people switch to electric vehicles, accessibility must be built into public charging from the start, not treated as an afterthought.

> helping drivers register where on-street charging is needed

> needs to become the standard

Register notes. `It's` uses a contraction, which does not appear in the release's own narration
anywhere in the sample: contractions live in quotes only. The middle sentence is the most
rhetorically constructed line found in the whole run, using a
**positive-assertion-then-negative-contrast** shape (`built into public charging from the
start, not treated as an afterthought`). And the argument is framed as a duty
(`must be built in`, `needs to become the standard`) rather than a sales point.

## Call to action (EXACT fragment)

> register their need for on-street residential EV charging at electricstreets.co.uk

The campaign CTA is `register`, and it asks for a civic action rather than a purchase. Note the
campaign domain is written bare and lowercase inline (`electricstreets.co.uk`) but with the
`www.` prefix in the boilerplate (`www.electricstreets.co.uk`).

## Statistic formatting (EXACT)

> 75% of councils · 15% of on-street chargers · 7% of councils

Numeral plus symbol, no space. Plain counts as numerals: `from 105 to 245`. Comma thousands
separator: `2,131 in London`. Standard cited as `PAS 1899:2022`, and the issuing body written
out in full as `British Standards Institution's`.

## Body coverage (FETCHER PARAPHRASE, structural only)

Vauxhall's **third annual Freedom of Information study** of UK councils. Responses from
`194 of 413 councils` surveyed `March–May 2026`; `49` now offer adapted or standards-compliant
charge points, roughly double the previous year; chargers in disabled bays have more than
doubled since 2024. Regional unevenness: London far ahead of the North West and Scotland. Also
covers cross-pavement partner `Kerbo Charge`, councils still permitting unsafe cable covers,
expected cross-pavement availability by end-2026, street nominations gathered to date, and
Vauxhall's `Motability Scheme` involvement, plus the Team GB partnership and electric line-up.

Note `March–May 2026` uses an en dash for the date range, and the survey base is given as
`194 of 413`, i.e. the brand publishes its own response rate rather than only the headline
number.
