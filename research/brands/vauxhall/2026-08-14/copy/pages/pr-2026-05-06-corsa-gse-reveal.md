## Headline (EXACT)

> NEW VAUXHALL CORSA GSE: ELECTRIFYING REBIRTH OF THE HOT HATCH

Colon construction. `ELECTRIFYING` is a deliberate double meaning (electric plus thrilling),
the only pun observed anywhere in the sample.

## Opening sentence (EXACT)

> Vauxhall has revealed the first pictures of the new Corsa GSE, as the brand makes its return to the hot hatch segment.

Third person, present perfect, brand as grammatical subject, news-first. This is the house
opening formula: subject `Vauxhall`, verb `has revealed` / `announces` / `opens orders`.

Two mechanics notes. First, the bullets use a **playful label before the colon**
(`Power aplenty:`, `Back to the future:`, `Hot hatch design:`) where the later pricing
release used a flat factual label. Second, `207Kw` here is **mis-capitalised** against
`207kW` in the 2026-08-05 release: the same figure, two spellings, three months apart. Also
`From 0 to 62mph` written out here versus `0-62mph` hyphenated in the pricing release. Unit
style is therefore **not rigorously enforced** between releases.

## Heritage and Britishness sentences (EXACT)

> Britain's best-selling small car

> The GSE brings the sporty genes of previous Vauxhall superminis back to life.

> Among the first Vauxhall hot hatches was the Nova GTE, launched in 1987 with a 100bhp 1.6-litre fuel-injected engine

> Following the Nova's facelift in 1990, the GTE version was renamed GSi.

> In 1993, the first Vauxhall Corsa was launched in Britain, and with it a GSi derivative.

> A five-speed gearbox, firmer suspension, bigger brakes and wider tyres turned the little Corsa into one of the very first 'pocket rockets'.

> 2007 saw the arrival of the Corsa VXR with a 1.6-litre turbocharged four-cylinder engine producing 189bhp

> A second-generation Corsa VXR was launched in 2015, based on the Corsa E, weighing in at 1,293kg.

> 2026 also marks Vauxhall's return to motorsport in the ADAC GSE Rally Cup

Two conventions established here. **Historic engines are quoted in `bhp`** (`100bhp`,
`189bhp`) while current powertrains are quoted in `PS` and `hp`; the brand does not
retro-fit modern units onto old cars. And **straight single quotes around a colloquialism**
(`'pocket rockets'`), which is how it licenses informal register without abandoning the
formal frame.
