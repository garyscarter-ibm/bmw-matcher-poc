## 1. Register, in three words

**Plain, warm, egalitarian.**

Justification, in that order.

**Plain.** The brand's own narration never reaches for a metaphor. Product releases open
`Vauxhall has revealed the first pictures of the new Corsa GSE, as the brand makes its return to
the hot hatch segment.` The Astra pack's 30 Key Facts bullets are `Label: detail` constructions
with no terminal punctuation and no adjectives beyond the functional. Where a competitor would
write a mood line, Vauxhall writes a number.

**Warm.** Warmth arrives through `proud` and `pleased`, not through jokes or intimacy.
`Vauxhall is proud to be the official automotive partner of Team GB.`,
`Vauxhall is proud to become the Official Automobile Partner...`,
`We are pleased to have delivered a strong first half of 2026...`,
`we are delighted to be involved`. It is the warmth of a long-established institution being
courteous, not the warmth of a brand trying to be your friend.

**Egalitarian.** This is the distinctive axis and it is heavily evidenced. `Attainable` is a
headline word (`NEW CORSA GSE – ATTAINABLE ELECTRIC HOT HATCH...`), a bullet label
(`Attainable: Prices start from £27,495 (On The Road)`), and a boilerplate concept. Beside it sit
`Accessible electric:`, `everyday accessibility`, `Price parity`,
`transactional price parity with equivalent diesel versions`,
`ELECTRIC FOR THE SAME MONTHLY PRICE AS DIESEL`, and the parent's
`electric equality for all`. The advocacy release argues that
`accessibility must be built into public charging from the start, not treated as an
afterthought.` Vauxhall's copy is organised around removing a barrier, not around aspiration.

Two secondary registers sit on top of the base:

- **GSE performance register.** Only when the subject is `GSE` does the vocabulary heat up:
  `ELECTRIFYING REBIRTH`, `Power aplenty`, `exhilarating performance`, `thrilling hot hatch`,
  `pocket rockets`, `an uncompromising stance`, `spectacular`. Even here the emotion is fenced
  into headlines, bullet labels and the Managing Director's quote, and the body reverts to
  figures.
- **Campaign and advocacy register.** The accessible-charging release publishes a statistic
  against its own sector and its own interest (`, BUT 75% OF COUNCILS STILL HAVE NO ACCESSIBLE
  CHARGERS`) and argues in the language of duty (`must be built into`, `needs to become the
  standard`). This is the closest the brand comes to a point of view.

What the register is **not**: not premium, not lyrical, not playful in its own narration, not
technical-boastful. There is no luxury vocabulary anywhere in the sample, and no attempt at wit
beyond a single pun (`ELECTRIFYING REBIRTH`) and two bullet labels (`Power aplenty`,
`Back to the future`).

## 2. Rhythm

Confidence: `medium` for structure, `low` for sentence-length figures. I could not count
sentences across a full body, so the word counts below are the fetcher's characterisation of one
release plus my own count over the short elements held exactly.

- **News-copy sentences run long and comma-heavy**, roughly 20 to 35 words, multiple clauses, no
  semicolons observed anywhere in the sample.
- **Quoted speech is markedly shorter**, roughly 8 to 15 words, and is where contractions
  (`It's`, `We're`) and rhetorical shape appear.
- **Bullets are fragments, not sentences.** They carry no terminal full stop, and they are the
  brand's dominant unit: the Astra pack opens with 30 of them, and every release opens with 4 to
  12. If you are writing Vauxhall copy, you are mostly writing bullets.
- **The bullet form is `Bold Label: detail`.** Labels are one or two words
  (`Heritage:`, `Charging:`, `Attainable:`, `Price parity:`, `Power aplenty:`).
- **Paragraph structure of a release is formulaic**: bullet summary, then body paragraphs of
  verified fact in third person, then a Managing Director quote carrying all interpretation and
  forward-looking language, then `ENDS`, then `ABOUT VAUXHALL MOTORS`. The results release of
  2026-07-06 runs seven factual paragraphs before its single quote.
- **Headlines are one clause, ALL CAPS, active, present tense, brand or person as subject.**
  Counted across 20 index items: `OPENS ORDERS FOR` x3, `ANNOUNCES`/`ANNOUNCED` x3, `REVEALS`
  x2, one each of `ACHIEVES`, `CELEBRATES`, `CLAIMS`, `SECURES`. No question marks, no exclamation
  marks, no second person, no `we`.
- **Headline length varies wildly**, from `VAUXHALL VANS: ELECTRIC FOR THE SAME MONTHLY PRICE AS
  DIESEL` to a 134-character advocacy headline. There is no evident character cap.

## 3. Person and address

- **Third person for the brand, always, in the brand's own narration.** `Vauxhall has revealed`,
  `Vauxhall announces`, `Vauxhall achieves`, `Vauxhall becomes`, `Vauxhall is proud to be`. Even
  the boilerplate uses `its philosophy is to build vehicles that...`, referring to itself
  possessively in the third person.
- **First person plural exists only inside attributed speech.** Every instance of `we` or `our`
  in the sample sits within a quotation: `as we bring the spirit of our iconic hot hatches into
  the electric era`, `We are pleased to have delivered...`, `These results reflect the strength
  of our product portfolio`, `our partnership with Team GB`.
- **Second person is almost absent.** Exactly one instance of `you` was found across 11
  artefacts: `You will not own the vehicle.`, a regulated Finance Lease disclosure. The App
  Store listing is the only artefact plausibly addressing a user directly, and its description
  could not be recovered verbatim, so second-person consumer address is **unsampled rather than
  absent**.
- **Imperatives are rare and confined to CTAs**: `Find out more at`, `Info:`,
  `Visit our product site`, `Visit our media site`, and the campaign's `register`. The rest of
  the corpus contains no commands.
- **Customers are `customers`, `drivers`, `motorists`, `retail customers`, `businesses`. Dealers
  are `Retailers`.** That last one is a firm house choice: `At Participating Retailers only.`,
  `Vauxhall Passenger Car Range on Private Retail channel`, and `retailer search` in the app.

## 4. Lexicon

### Reached for repeatedly

**Access and price**: `attainable`, `accessible`, `affordable`, `price parity`, `saving`,
`from just £`, `Prices start from`, `for the same monthly price as`, `deposit contribution`,
`Electric Car Grant`, `equality`.

**Electrification**: `electric`, `fully electric`, `all-electric`, `battery-electric`,
`electrifying`, `Electric`, `electrification`, `make the switch`, `the electric transition`,
`on-street charging`, `range`, `WLTP`.

**Britishness**: `Britain`, `Britain's`, `British`, `the UK`, `UK's`, `1903`,
`the oldest and most familiar of Britain's automotive manufacturers`, `Ellesmere Port`,
`Team GB`, `Britain's businesses`, `Electric Streets of Britain`, `GB Mainland`.

**Institutional warmth**: `proud`, `pleased`, `delighted`, `committed to`, `support`,
`partnership`, `iconic`, `heritage`, `tradition`, `revives`, `legacy`, `genes`.

**Leaderboard proof**: `best-selling`, `most powerful`, `fastest accelerating`,
`market share`, `year-to-date`, `class-leading`, `unique in the market`, `first`, `only`,
`one of few`.

**Structural intensifier**: `just` does double duty, on price (`for just £131 per month`,
`a customer deposit of just £2,000`, `from just £34,495 OTR`), on time
(`in just 12 months`, `in just 32 minutes`) and on margin (`by just 8.5 seconds`).

**Hedge words**: `up to` (pervasive, on range, boot space, payload, value, economy),
`approximately`, `from`, `eligible for`, `available`.

### Obviously avoided

Observed absences across 11 artefacts. Each is an absence in the sampled corpus, not a proven
brand prohibition, since the consumer register is unread.

- **No luxury or premium vocabulary at all.** No `luxury`, `premium`, `sophisticated`,
  `crafted`, `exclusive`, `refined`, `indulgent`, `prestige`.
- **No exclamation marks anywhere**, in any register, including offers.
- **No questions in headlines or body.** The only question mark in the corpus is inside the
  proper noun `What Car?`.
- **No `e-` product prefix.** Powertrain is a postfixed proper noun (`Astra Electric`,
  `Vivaro Electric`, `Combo Electric`). Nothing named `e-Corsa` or `e-Astra` exists in the
  sample.
- **No `YTD`, no `H1`.** Written out as `year-to-date` and `first half of 2026`.
- **No `dealer`.** `Retailer` throughout.
- **No `km`, no `l/100km`.** Miles, mph, mpg for consumer-facing figures.
- **No semicolons** observed in any release.
- **No em dashes** observed. The spaced en dash `–` does that work. Recorded with the caveat that
  markdown conversion could in principle normalise dash characters, though the coexistence of
  `–` and `-` in different headlines suggests it did not.
- **No slogan sign-off.** No release in the sample ends on a tagline. `Energising a Better
  Britain` appears on the parent's site and in none of the 10 Vauxhall press artefacts.

### Product naming architecture

- Model plus powertrain as a proper noun: `Astra Electric`, `Astra Plug-in Hybrid`,
  `Astra Hybrid`, `Astra Petrol`, `Combo Electric`, `Vivaro Electric`, `Movano Electric`,
  `Frontera Electric`, `New Grandland Electric`.
- Performance sub-brand `GSE`, always uppercase, always postfixed: `Corsa GSE`, `Mokka GSE`,
  `Mokka GSE Rally`. Historic equivalents `GTE`, `GSi`, `VXR` follow the same postfix rule.
- Trims are single capitalised words: `Griffin`, `GS`, `Ultimate`, `Pro`. Set ALL CAPS only as
  spec-block headings.
- Technology families are `Pure` plus a capitalised noun, mostly closed up:
  `PureTechnology`, `Vauxhall PureEnergy`, `Vauxhall PureSense`, `Vauxhall PureConnect`.
- Driver assistance is `Intelli-` plus a hyphen: `Intelli-Lux HD`, `Intelli-Drive 1.0`,
  `Intelli-Seats`, `Intelli-HUD`, `Intelli-LED`.
- Design language is `Bold & Pure`, with an ampersand.
- `New` behaves as a persistent product prefix, not a passing adjective: `NEW CORSA GSE`,
  `NEW MOKKA YES`, `NEW ASTRA`, `NEW GRANDLAND GRIFFIN`, `New Grandland Electric`.

## 5. Mechanics

Confidence `high`: all of this is directly observed in exactly-reproduced short elements.

**Casing.** ALL CAPS for press headlines, `ENDS`, `ABOUT VAUXHALL MOTORS`, trim-block headings
(`GRIFFIN`, `ULTIMATE`), the app's image disclaimer, and embargo labels. Sentence case is the
default for editorial section headings (`Over 45 years of heritage`, `Design`, `Charging`). Title
Case appears inconsistently for a minority of headings (`Key Facts`, `Comfort and Practicality`,
`New Vauxhall`, `Electric Streets`) and for proper-noun partner titles
(`Official Automobile Partner`). Technology-family headings take the pattern
`PureSense: Intelligent safety`, i.e. brand noun, colon, sentence-case benefit.

**Punctuation.**
- Spaced en dash `–` for asides and ranges. Also used as a headline separator
  (`NEW CORSA GSE – ATTAINABLE...`), while a spaced hyphen `-` does the same job in another
  headline eleven days earlier (`VAUXHALL ASTRA & ASTRA SPORTS TOURER - PRESS INFORMATION`).
- Colon is the preferred headline separator (`VAUXHALL VANS: ELECTRIC FOR...`,
  `NEW VAUXHALL CORSA GSE: ELECTRIFYING REBIRTH...`) and the universal bullet separator.
- Ampersand used freely: headlines, job titles, bullet text (`Heated front seats & steering
  wheel`, `front & rear parking sensors`), sub-heads (`Comfort & convenience:`), table labels
  (`inc. ECG & Sales Event offer`), `T&Cs`, `Bold & Pure`, `stop & go`.
- Oxford comma **used, but not universally**. Present in `Griffin, GS, and top-of-the-range
  Ultimate`, `Corsa GSi, and Corsa VXR`, `battery management, and more`,
  `fully electric, plug-in hybrid, 48V hybrid, and 1.2-litre petrol options`,
  `exceptional performance, handling stability, and direct feedback`. Absent in
  `excludes NI, IoM and Channel Islands` and several bullets.
- Curly apostrophes and single quotes in the Astra pack (`driver’s`, `‘Carbon Black’`); straight
  single quotes around a colloquialism elsewhere (`'pocket rockets'`).
- `+` and `=` used as operators inside bullets (`electric + massage seats`,
  `100kW (DC) 20-80% = 32m`).
- No exclamation marks, no semicolons, no em dashes observed.
- Bullets take no terminal punctuation. Body sentences do.

**Numerals and units.** Units close up to the numeral with no space, without exception:
`281PS`, `207kW`, `345Nm`, `58kWh`, `100kW`, `355mm`, `4,374mm`, `1,733` kg in a `(kg)` column,
`105mph`, `48.7mpg`, `759kg`, `18-inch`, `52kWh`. Thousands take a comma (`1,634 litres`,
`2,131`, `1,293kg`, `50,000 Tesco Clubcard points`). Percentages close up (`75%`, `4.8%`, `0%
APR`). Times as `3h 20min`, `5h45m`, `32 minutes`, `8 hours`. Ratios as `40:20:40`, `60/40`.
Temperature as `60 degrees`, word not symbol. Volume as `cubic metres` written out, and
`litres` for boot space. Aerodynamics as `0.269 (cd)`.

**Power units are mixed by design.** `PS` in spec tables, `hp` in prose, `kW` parenthetically
after `PS`, and `bhp` reserved for historic engines (`100bhp` on a 1987 Nova, `189bhp` on a 2007
Corsa VXR). The brand does not retro-fit modern units onto old cars.

**Measurement system.** Imperial for the consumer-facing dimensions: miles of range, mph, mpg,
miles per year, `miles/kWh`. Metric for engineering: mm, kg, Nm, kWh, kW, g/km, litres, metres.
No kilometres anywhere.

**Currency.** `£` closed up to the numeral. Round figures unpenced in body and bullets
(`£1,000`, `£32,995`, `£27,495`). Finance tables always to two decimals (`£27,045.00`,
`£130.93`). Sub-unit as `7p per kWh`. `OTR` and `On The Road Price` both in use, the latter
Title Cased in full. VAT status bracketed and both spellings occur: `(incl. VAT)`,
`(excl. VAT)`.

**Dates. Five formats coexist**, by register:
1. Index and dateline: `12 Aug 2026`, `13 Jul 2026`.
2. Editorial body and bullets: `Saturday 18 July`, `March–May 2026`.
3. Offer prose and small print: `8th to 31st May`, `30th June 2026`,
   `1st July 2025 to 30th June 2026`, `17th-18th July`.
4. Contractual terms: `08/05/2026 and 31/05/2026`.
5. Embargo furniture: `Wednesday, 22nd July @ 00:01 (UK)`.

**Spelling.** UK throughout: `colours`, `centre`, `kerb`, `callipers`, `tyres`, `line-up`.

**Legal and hedging conventions.**
- Asterisk for a single caveat, and the caveat can be extremely terse: the entire hedge on the
  headline price `£32,995` is `*Pending government approval`.
- Numbered footnote markers ride on the last word with no space (`...new retail car customers2`)
  and the note repeats the bare digit (`2Available across...`).
- Table-level asterisks explained in the header (`OTR price * After ECG`) and applied to values
  (`£29,995 / £28,495*`).
- WLTP qualifies every range and economy figure, inline in brackets rather than in small print.
- `up to` precedes essentially every performance or capacity figure.
- Superlatives are narrowed by a qualifier instead of a footnote:
  `UK's best-selling supermini year-to-date`, `Most powerful production Corsa of all time`,
  `Fastest accelerating Vauxhall on sale`, `one of few automotive brands to produce electric
  vehicles in Britain`. The qualifier is the compliance mechanism.
- Offer small print is exhaustive and granular, down to naming a non-participating retail group
  (`Arnold Clark sites are not participating retailers.`), household limits
  (`Only 1 claim per household permitted.`), and geographic carve-outs
  (`Offer excludes NI, IoM and Channel Islands.`).
- Regulated finance disclosures verbatim: `You will not own the vehicle.`,
  `a credit broker and not a lender`, `subject to status`.
- `T&Cs apply.` is the abbreviated form.

## 6. What they lead on

Ranked by frequency and by structural position across the 11 artefacts.

1. **Access and affordability.** The most consistent theme, and the one carried in the
   boilerplate's philosophy sentence. It appears as price, as monthly cost, as parity with
   diesel, as a grant, and as `attainable` used as a category label alongside `Heritage` and
   `Powertrains`.
2. **Electrification as a public project.** Not "our EVs are advanced" but "electric should work
   for everyone, including on your street and including if you are a disabled driver." The
   `Electric Streets of Britain` campaign and the annual FOI study are the load-bearing proof.
3. **Britishness and longevity.** `1903`, `the oldest and most familiar of Britain's automotive
   manufacturers`, `Ellesmere Port`, `Team GB`. Note the framing is **familiarity**, not
   prestige: `most familiar`, `most loved sports team`, `Britain's best-selling small car`.
4. **Leaderboard proof.** Market share, best-selling claims, third-party survey placings. Value
   is asserted through popularity rather than through quality adjectives.
5. **Capability, but only under the GSE badge**, and even then framed as
   `exhilarating performance with everyday accessibility`.
6. **Technology, named rather than explained.** The `Pure*` and `Intelli-*` families are listed
   as facts in bullets; the copy rarely explains what they do for the reader in feeling terms.

Notably **absent as a lead**: driving pleasure as a general proposition, design as an emotional
argument, sustainability as a moral argument (it appears as a bullet label, `Sustainable:`, not
as a theme), and any status or self-image appeal.

## 9. Do and don't, drawn only from evidence

Every line below points to something quoted in `phrase-bank.md`. Nothing here is inferred from
general automotive practice.

### Do

- Write the brand in the third person. `Vauxhall announces`, `Vauxhall opens orders for`,
  `Vauxhall is proud to`.
- Put `we` and `our` only in an attributed quote.
- Lead a product story with a bullet summary in `Label: detail` form, no terminal full stops.
- Give affordability the same structural weight as performance. `Attainable:` is a legitimate
  bullet label beside `Powertrains:`.
- Use `attainable`, `accessible`, `price parity`, `from just £`, `up to`.
- Close up units to numerals: `281PS`, `58kWh`, `0-62mph`, `1,634 litres`.
- Keep consumer-facing distance and speed imperial: miles, mph, mpg.
- Qualify every range and economy figure with a bracketed WLTP label next to the number.
- Narrow a superlative with a qualifier rather than a footnote:
  `UK's best-selling supermini year-to-date`.
- Name the powertrain as a postfixed proper noun: `Vivaro Electric`, `Astra Plug-in Hybrid`.
- Keep `GSE` uppercase and after the model name.
- Say `Retailer`, not dealer.
- Sign off with `Find out more at www.electricstreets.co.uk` and `Info: www.vauxhall.co.uk`.
- Use `proud` for a partnership and `pleased` for a result.
- Say `Britain` and `Britain's` when you mean the country in a brand sentence, and `the UK` in a
  factual or market sentence.
- Let a campaign publish an inconvenient number, as the 2026-07-29 headline does.
- Close a release with `ENDS` above `ABOUT VAUXHALL MOTORS`.

### Don't

- Don't use exclamation marks. None appear in 11 artefacts.
- Don't ask the reader a question in a headline.
- Don't reach for premium or luxury vocabulary. There is none in the corpus.
- Don't write `e-Corsa`, `e-Astra` or any `e-` prefix. It does not exist in the sample.
- Don't abbreviate to `YTD` or `H1`. Write `year-to-date` and `first half of 2026`.
- Don't use kilometres for a consumer figure.
- Don't use an em dash. Use the spaced en dash `–`.
- Don't use a semicolon. None appear.
- Don't put emotion in the brand's own narration; put it in the quote.
- Don't end a release on a slogan. No sampled release does.
- Don't treat `Energising a Better Britain` as a Vauxhall sign-off. It is the parent's line for
  Vauxhall and appears in none of the 10 Vauxhall press artefacts.
- Don't put a full stop on a bullet.
- Don't assume the boilerplate is fixed: the Team GB sentence is present on some releases and
  absent from the 2026-07-13 pack.

### Where the brand is inconsistent, and you must pick
