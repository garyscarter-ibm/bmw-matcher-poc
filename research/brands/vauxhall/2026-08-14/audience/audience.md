# Target audience — Vauxhall (Vauxhall Motors Limited, UK) — collected 2026-08-14

**Rung landed on: 2 for portfolio segment role and price-band strategy; 3 for every
segment-specific finding; 4 (SMMT) for third-party volume corroboration.**
Rung 1 (published segmentation or media kit with audience data) was searched for and

## 0. Method notes that affect how you read this file

1. **Rung 2 was opened, and it paid.** Recon never got past the investor hub page. The

   environment.
3. **Verbatim capture was partly blocked by the fetcher, not by the source.** On several
   pages the fetching model declined to reproduce text beyond about 125 characters per
   quotation and returned a paraphrase instead. Where a wording below is paraphrase-derived

4. Nothing prompt-injection-like, hostile or self-referential was found in any fetched

## 1. Segments

Four segments have evidence. They are ordered by the weight of evidence behind them, not
by assumed volume.

### Segment A. Value-led private retail buyer, supermini and small SUV

**Who they are.** UK private buyers at the bottom of the mainstream price band, buying a
B-segment hatch or a small crossover, typically on finance. Vauxhall does not publish
their demographics and no third-party demographic profile was found (see Section 5).

**What they are buying it for.** A known, familiar, low-monthly-cost car with a lot of
standard kit for the money. The brand's own boilerplate frames the job as vehicles
"relevant to all" offering design, equipment, technology and "electrification at an
attainable level" `[paraphrase]` (s-a5, s-a6, s-a8, s-a9).

**Decision drivers, evidenced.**
- Monthly cost, stated as such. The May 2026 sales event translates its own discount into
  roughly "£20 per month over a typical four-year finance agreement" (s-a7).
- Spec for money. The new Mokka YES trim replaces Griffin at the foot of the range and is
  sold on "enhanced specification and distinctive styling at an attainable price", adding
  parking sensors, a 180-degree reversing camera, blind-spot detection, keyless entry and
  power-folding heated mirrors without a price rise (s-a9).
- Named entry price. Mokka range opens at £26,545 OTR (s-a9); Astra at £27,495 OTR
  (s-a8); a Corsa YES 1.2 Turbo 100PS is quoted at £20,890 OTR, £19,390 after event and
  online-store savings (s-a7). £20,890 is the lowest OTR figure found anywhere this run.
- Third-party corroboration of the segment's centrality: SMMT records Corsa as the 5th
  best-selling car in the UK for Jan to Jul 2026 at 20,796 units, and the 10th best seller
  in July alone at 2,326 (s-a12).

**Objections the brand actively answers.**
- "Electric costs more." Answered by inverting it: Astra Electric is presented under the
  heading "Accessible electric" as costing less than the hybrid and PHEV versions once the
  £1,500 Electric Car Grant applies (s-a8).
- "I have nowhere to charge." Answered by the Electric Streets of Britain campaign and by
  £500 credit toward an Ohme home charger, a Kerbo Charge cross-pavement channel, or public
  charging credit (s-a7, s-a5).
- "Will it be any good." Answered with third-party endorsement: Grandland 3rd, Frontera 5th
  and Mokka 8th in the Auto Express Driver Power 2026 survey (s-a3, headline dated
  2026-06-10).

**Price sensitivity: high, and the brand behaves as though it is very high.** Stacked
incentives observed in a single May 2026 event: a £1,000 extra discount on any model, a
£1,500 Electric Car Grant, a £500 charging credit, 10,000 miles of free home charging
(valued at £175), and 50,000 Tesco Clubcard points valued at £500 (s-a7). A grocery
loyalty-scheme tie-in is a mass-market channel choice, not a premium one.

**Evidence that the segment exists:** first-party pricing and trim architecture (s-a7,
s-a8, s-a9), first-party volume claim (s-a4), third-party registration data (s-a12).
Confidence: high.

### Segment B. SME and sole-trader van buyer

**Who they are.** Business customers buying one or a few light commercial vehicles. The
brand names them explicitly and repeatedly as "Retail (SME) business customers" and sole
traders, and states the offer "applies to Business customers only" `[paraphrase]` (s-a6).
No large-fleet programme is described in that release.

**What they are buying it for.** A working van at a monthly cost that does not penalise
choosing electric. The release headline is the whole proposition: "VAUXHALL VANS: ELECTRIC
FOR THE SAME MONTHLY PRICE AS DIESEL" (s-a3, s-a6).

**Decision drivers, evidenced.** Deliberate monthly-payment parity between diesel and
electric on three named finance products (s-a6):
- LEASYS Business Contract Hire, 36 months, 10,000 miles a year: Combo Pro diesel 100hp
  and Combo Electric 136hp both £305 a month; Vivaro Prime diesel 120hp and Vivaro
  Electric 136hp both £335 a month.
- Stellantis Financial Services Conditional Sale, 60 months, 6.9% fixed, 6.9%
  representative APR: Combo Prime-Plus £299 a month in both fuels; Vivaro Prime £339 a
  month in both.
- SFS Finance Lease, 36 months: Combo Pro £289 a month in both; Vivaro Prime £319 a month
  in both.
Plus payload and load volume (Combo to 4.4m3 and 759kg; Vivaro to 6.6m3 and 1,210kg), WLTP
range (Combo 213 miles, Vivaro 219 miles), a free Ohme charger with installation worth up
to £1,050 including VAT, and a £1,000 "Professional Days" contribution (s-a6).

**Objections.** Total cost of ownership versus diesel (answered by the parity structure),
range, and charging (answered by the bundled charger). Note the release states Vauxhall
Motors Limited acts as a credit broker introducing only Stellantis Financial Services and
receives no commission, while retailers may `[paraphrase]` (s-a6): a compliance-driven
disclosure that tells you finance is the primary purchase route for this segment.

**Price sensitivity: high, and expressed in monthly payment rather than list price.** This
matters, and it is the sharpest claimed-versus-observed finding in the dimension. See
Section 2, divergence 2.

**Evidence that the segment exists:** first-party targeting language and finance tables
(s-a6); first-party volume claim of 13,838 LCVs and 8.3% LCV share in H1 2026 (s-a4);
third-party SMMT LCV registration data of 15,015 units and 8.02% share for Jan to Jul 2026
(s-a13). Confidence: high for the segment, medium for its trajectory (see divergence 4).

### Segment C. Motability and accessibility-constrained driver

**Who they are.** Disabled drivers and their families acquiring a car through the
Motability Scheme, and more broadly drivers dependent on on-street public charging because
they have no off-street parking. The 2026-07-29 release also names elderly residents,
wheelchair users and pedestrians as beneficiaries `[paraphrase]` (s-a5).

**What they are buying it for.** A vehicle obtainable within Scheme rules, in a body style
that fits an access need, that can actually be charged. Five Scheme-eligible electric
models are named: Corsa Electric, Mokka Electric, Astra Electric, Combo Life Electric and
Vivaro Life Electric (the last seating up to nine) `[paraphrase]` (s-a5).

**Decision drivers, evidenced.** Scheme eligibility; long institutional relationship
(Vauxhall states it is a founding member of the Motability Scheme and has worked with the
Motability Foundation and Motability Operations since 1977) `[paraphrase]`; and demonstrated
Scheme standing (Vauxhall states it was the Scheme's best-selling manufacturer in 2024)
`[paraphrase]` (s-a5).

**Objections, and this is the one the brand campaigns on.** Charging inaccessibility.
Vauxhall's own Freedom of Information research, fieldwork March to May 2026, sent to 413
UK councils with 194 replying, reports: 49 councils have accessible on-street charging in
2026 against 24 in 2025; on-street chargers in disabled bays up from 105 in 2024 to 245;
about 15% of on-street chargers adapted for disabled drivers; 14% meeting BSI PAS
1899:2022; and over half of responding councils factoring disabled and elderly residents
into charging strategy. An earlier round (fieldwork November 2025 to January 2026, tier-one
councils) found 7% of councils still permit unsafe cable covers and 42% will offer
cross-pavement charging by end of 2026. Campaign registrations: over 12,500 streets
nominated and over 500 respondents stating a need for disabled-access parking
`[paraphrase]` (s-a5).

**Price sensitivity: structurally different.** Motability purchases are mediated by an
allowance rather than by list price, and the brand treats the channel as separate: the
Tesco Clubcard element of the May 2026 sales event is explicitly restricted to retail
customers and excludes Motability, fleet and LCV (s-a7). That exclusion is itself the
cleanest evidence that Motability is a distinct channel in Vauxhall's own commercial
model, not a slice of retail.

**Evidence that the segment exists:** first-party Motability statements and model list,
first-party commissioned FOI research across three years, and a first-party channel
exclusion in offer terms (s-a5, s-a7). Confidence: medium-high. All Motability figures are
brand claims reaching me in paraphrase, and none was independently corroborated this run.

### Segment D. Company-car and fleet driver

**Who they are.** Drivers taking a Vauxhall through an employer scheme, and the fleet
buyers who specify it. Evidence is thinner than for A to C but it is present and explicit.

**What they are buying it for.** Low benefit-in-kind exposure and high-mileage
suitability. The Astra press information states that the electric version's added chassis
stiffness and stability suit long motorway runs "for company car users covering
significant mileage", and publishes BiK rates (4% electric, 10% PHEV, 28% hybrid, 32%
petrol) alongside P11D and VED tables (s-a8).

**Decision drivers, evidenced.** BiK percentage, P11D value, VED, WLTP range (Astra
Electric 281 miles hatch, 275 Sports Tourer), and DC charging 20 to 80% in 32 minutes
(s-a3, s-a8). Pan-European leasing capability exists at group level through Leasys, a 50%
joint venture with Credit Agricole Consumer Finance & Mobility for multi-brand long-term
operational leasing (s-a2, p26).

**Objections.** Range and charge time for high-mileage drivers, answered with the figures
above.

**Price sensitivity: mediated by tax, not by sticker price.** The published BiK ladder is
the argument.

**Evidence that the segment exists:** first-party company-car reference and tax tables
(s-a8); Vauxhall breaking out retail growth (+19%) separately in its own results release,
which implies a non-retail remainder it chooses not to size (s-a4). Market-wide context
only, third party: SMMT records the July 2026 UK new-car market as 37.1% private, 59.9%
fleet, 3.0% business (s-a12). **That is the whole market, not Vauxhall.** Vauxhall's own
retail-versus-fleet split is NOT PUBLISHED. Confidence: medium for the segment existing,
none for its size.

### What the investor filing says, which is the strongest corroboration available

The 2025 Annual Report and Form 20-F, produced 2026-02-26, places Vauxhall in writing:

- Five portfolios are named: luxury (Maserati); premium (Alfa Romeo, DS, Lancia); global
  SUV (Jeep); American brands (Dodge, Ram, Chrysler); and "European brands covering
  Abarth, Citroen, FIAT, Opel, Peugeot and Vauxhall vehicles" (s-a2, p12).
- The Enlarged Europe segment is sold "under the mainstream brands Citroen, FIAT, Opel,
  Peugeot, Vauxhall as well as premium brands Alfa Romeo, DS and Lancia" (s-a2, p16).

So the group itself classifies Vauxhall as **mainstream, explicitly not premium and not
luxury**, in a document written for shareholders. This is the load-bearing audience fact
of the dimension, and it is rung 2, first party, A2, dated.

Group price strategy corroborates the direction of travel: "in order to address an actual
or perceived affordability issue in our product portfolio, we are launching several new
models at lower price points. This may adversely affect mix in future periods" (s-a2, p47).
The strategic reassessment is described as informed by "customer purchasing behavior,
affordability considerations, infrastructure readiness and incentive frameworks" (s-a2,
p14 and p52).

Regulatory context for the UK audience, stated in the same filing: "The UK implemented a
regulation beginning in 2024 with obligations for manufacturers to achieve a minimum
percentage of ZEVs increasing each year and reaching 100 percent in 2035, and specific
targets on CO2 for non-ZEV CO2 fleet", with credit banking, borrowing and trading
flexibilities (s-a2, p39). The filing does not use the phrase "ZEV mandate".

### What is observed

**Observation 1: the price band matches the mainstream claim.** Every OTR figure captured
this run sits between £20,890 (Corsa YES) and £38,105 (Mokka Ultimate Electric). The
performance halo tops out at £36,995 (Mokka GSE). No Vauxhall above £40,000 appeared in
any of the eight releases read. Consistent with the claim.

**Observation 2: sponsorship is mass-participation, without exception.** A dated pattern,
not a snapshot:
- Football Conference (England's top non-league division) 1986 to 1998, succeeding Gola and
  succeeded by Nationwide Building Society (s40, re-verified this run, citing The Guardian
  2011-01-10 and BBC News 2011-01-11).
- Primary sponsor of the England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales national football
  teams from 2011 (same sources).
- Official Automotive Partner of Team GB, supporting athletes toward the Los Angeles 2028
  Olympic Games (s-a4, s-a5, s-a6, s-a10). Term and value NOT PUBLISHED.
- Official Automobile Partner of the Novuna London Athletics Meet, London Stadium,
  18 July 2026 (s-a3).
- ADAC GSE Rally Cup with an electric Mokka GSE Rally, driver Ioan Lloyd (s-a3, s-a10).

Non-league football, the four home nations, an Olympic team and a public athletics meet are
national and participatory. No golf, sailing, motorsport-at-F1-level, arts or luxury
partnership was found. **Implication, stated as implication and not as fact:** the revealed
preference is for reach across the whole national population rather than for signalling
status to a narrow one. That is consistent with the mainstream classification.

**Observation 3: the "British brand" claim is a marketing and manufacturing claim more
than a product one.** The filing shows Vauxhall Motors Limited as a 100%-owned UK
subsidiary of Stellantis NV (s-a2, p378), sharing a chief executive with Opel (Florian
Huettl, titled "CEO, Opel and Vauxhall", s19), and describes the products as "the Opel and
Vauxhall Mokka", "Opel/Vauxhall Combo", "Opel/Vauxhall Frontera" and "Opel/Vauxhall Corsa"
(s-a2, pp18, 25, 67). One UK plant appears in the group's plant list: Ellesmere Port
(s-a2, p495), which the brand describes as the UK's first electric-only manufacturing plant
and the home of Combo Electric `[paraphrase]` (s-a5, s-a6). Britishness is therefore true
of the company, the market and one factory, and not of the engineering, which is shared with
a pan-European twin. Recorded as a divergence between claim and structure, not as a
contradiction.

### Divergences, each recorded and none resolved silently

**Divergence 1: "electric equality for all" versus the observed emphasis on performance
derivatives.** Of the 20 press releases on the index (2026-04-29 to 2026-08-12), seven
concern GSE performance or motorsport (Corsa GSE twice, Mokka GSE press information, Mokka
GSE Rally reveal, and three ADAC GSE Rally Cup results) against one release on the
entry-level YES trim. The claimed positioning is access; the observed communications mix
over these three and a half months leans halo. Both are first-party. Do not treat the
claim as describing the output.

**Divergence 2: "attainable" as a price claim versus as a finance claim.** In the van
release the electric list prices carry large premiums over diesel (Combo Prime-Plus
£34,569 against £29,917, a £4,652 premium; Vivaro Prime £47,075 against £37,291, a £9,784
premium), and yet the monthly payments are identical (£299 and £339 respectively). Parity
is engineered in the finance structure and a £1,000 contribution, not in the price of the
vehicle (s-a6). The audience being addressed is one that buys a monthly payment. This is
the most consequential divergence for how the brand can honestly talk about affordability.

**Divergence 3: the market-share claim rests on a combined base.** Vauxhall's own release
of 2026-07-06 is headlined "VAUXHALL ACHIEVES TOTAL MARKET SHARE OF 4.8% IN FIRST HALF OF
2026", with 62,839 vehicles registered in H1 2026, combined car and van volume up 7% year
to date, retail up 19%, and a climb from sixth to fourth in the combined car and van market
(s-a4). Third-party SMMT data for a different and longer period, Jan to Jul 2026, gives
Vauxhall **cars only** 54,841 units at 4.24% share (down from 4.36% a year earlier), and
**LCVs** 15,015 units at 8.02% share (s-a12, s-a13). The bases differ (combined versus
car-only) and the periods differ (six months versus seven), so these are not in
contradiction, but the headline 4.8% is a combined figure and reads higher than the
car-market share a consumer would recognise. **Both figures are recorded with their bases
and periods. They are not averaged and not reconciled.**

**Divergence 4: the growth narrative versus the latest month.** Vauxhall's H1 release
claims LCV volume up 14%, 1,731 more units, to 13,838, with share up 0.82 points to 8.3%
(s-a4). SMMT for Jan to Jul 2026 shows Vauxhall LCVs at 15,015, up 6.03%, at 8.02% share
(s-a13). SMMT for July 2026 alone shows Vauxhall LCVs **down 39.72%** year on year (1,264
against 2,097) with share roughly halved from 8.95% to 4.42% (s-a13). Car side, July 2026
was up 19.38% (5,840 against 4,892) with share up to 3.73% (s-a12). Periods differ and a
single month is volatile, so this is flagged as unresolved tension between the brand's
published trajectory and the most recent third-party month, not as a refutation.

**Divergence 5: the third-party sponsorship record is stale.** Wikipedia's Sponsorships
section documents only the Football Conference era and the 2011 home-nations deal, and
carries nothing on Team GB, LA 2028, the Novuna London Athletics Meet or the ADAC GSE Rally
Cup (s40, verified this run). The current sponsorship set exists only in first-party
releases. Anyone building the audience picture from third-party sources alone would get a
15-year-old answer.

**Divergence 6: brand-commissioned research is not audience data, and should not be used as
such.** The 2026-08-12 release reports an OnePoll survey of 2,000 UK adults, nationally
representative, fieldwork June 2026: 26% think they could qualify for LA2028; 24%
"quietly confident" about sporting ability; 33% rank confidence above natural talent; 39%
rate their own driving "better than average", rising to 49% of men; 38% of men rate
themselves better than average at sport against 18% of women; 67% perform better with
friendly competition, 74% of men (s-a10). This is PR research about sporting and driving
self-confidence among UK adults generally. It is **not** a profile of Vauxhall buyers, it
carries no age or regional splits, and it must not be repurposed as segmentation.

## 3. Register implications

Cross-referenced conceptually to the copy dimension; its files were not read and its work
is not duplicated here.

1. **Money is the message, and it is stated monthly.** Prices appear as "from £X OTR",
   savings as round pounds, and cost as a monthly payment ("£20 per month over a typical
   four-year finance agreement", "£299 a month"). Copy for this audience must carry
   numbers, carry them precisely, and carry the qualifying terms nearby, because the
   propositions are legally conditioned (APR, deposit, final payment, excess mileage,
   registration deadlines, geographic exclusions).
2. **"Attainable" and "accessible" are the brand's own load-bearing words**, appearing in
   trim positioning, model headings ("Accessible electric"), and boilerplate. "Premium" is
   used as a negative, as in buyers getting extra space "without paying a premium" (s-a8).
   The register is therefore value-affirming rather than status-conferring.
3. **The audience cannot be assumed to have a driveway.** An entire campaign exists
   because a material part of the addressable market charges at the kerb (s-a5). Copy that
   presumes home charging excludes people the brand is explicitly courting.
4. **A part of the audience is disabled, and the brand addresses them directly rather than
   euphemistically.** Its own research language is plain: disabled drivers, disabled bays,
   accessible chargers, cross-pavement channels. Register should match that plainness.
5. **National-sport register, not elite register.** Non-league football through to Team GB
   is a register of participation and of "us", not of aspiration to exclusivity.
6. **UK-only means UK conventions throughout**, with no market variants to reconcile: OTR,
   BiK, P11D, VED, Electric Car Grant, Motability, mpg, miles, £. Several of these are
   nouns no other Stellantis brand's UK-adjacent copy needs to explain.
7. **Trim names are words, not codes.** YES, Griffin, Design, GS, Ultimate, GSE. Note that
   the brand nowhere explains what YES stands for (s-a9): a naming decision the audience is
   expected to absorb rather than decode.
8. **Two owner-facing registers coexist and do not match.** MyVauxhall is written for
   "Vauxhall drivers" broadly and rates 4.49 from 16,142 ratings; MyVauxhall 2.0 is an
   early preview restricted to one model, New Grandland Electric, tiered CONNECT ONE and
   CONNECT PLUS, and rates 3.81 from 311 ratings (s-a11). The tier names are the brand
   segmenting its own owners by subscription, and the ratings gap is a published signal
   about the newer tiered experience. Treat the counts as scale indicators only.

## 4. Geography

**United Kingdom only.** Evidence gathered this run:
- The brand is described as "The British automotive brand" and the tagline names Britain
  (s19).
- The group filing lists Vauxhall solely within the Enlarged Europe segment, and Vauxhall
  Motors Limited as a United Kingdom entity, 100% owned (s-a2, pp16, 378).
- All press output is on a `uk-en` locale path and all prices are in £ OTR (s-a3).
- The one UK plant in the group's plant list is Ellesmere Port (s-a2, p495).

**Note on the withdrawal date.** The run brief states Vauxhall was withdrawn from all
non-UK markets in the early 1980s. I did not find a source for that this run and I am not
asserting it. Recorded as unverified here.

**Audience consequences worth stating.** Two follow directly from the evidence:
1. The brand carries no international audience, so every positioning, sponsorship and
   register decision is calibrated to exactly one national market, and can be. That is why
   national sport works as a strategy and why the copy can assume shared UK reference points
   without translation cost.
2. The audience is national but the product is not. The filing names the vehicles as
   Opel/Vauxhall pairs and gives the two brands one chief executive (s-a2, s19). The brand
   therefore has to make UK-specific meaning out of pan-European hardware, which is exactly
   what the Britishness, Ellesmere Port, Team GB and Electric Streets material is doing.

**Regional skew within the UK: NOT ESTABLISHED.** No customer-distribution data by region
was found at any rung. The only regional figures located are charging infrastructure, not
customers: 43 accessible on-street chargers in the Northwest, 14 in Scotland, 2,131 in
London (s-a5). Two weak operational signals point at a GB-mainland addressable audience for
some propositions: the Ohme charger offer is GB mainland only, and the Octopus charging
credit excludes Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands (s-a6, s-a7).
These are offer mechanics, not audience geography, and should not be read as either.
