# Performance sub-brand: GSE, and the status of VXR

Rung 2 (first-party dated press releases). Confidence: high for the spelling and the
expansion, medium for the VXR position.

## DISAGREEMENT WITH THIS RUN'S BRIEF, not resolved silently

The brief for this run states the current performance line is written **"GSe"**. Every
first-party body-text occurrence collected here writes it **"GSE"**, capital E.

- s53 (New Vauxhall Mokka GSE press information, 2026-07-13): the fetcher reports the
  badge "appears throughout as 'GSE' (capital E), never 'GSe'", and that the release
  defines it twice, as "GSE: Grand Sport Electric." and as "Vauxhall introduced its new
  GSE (Grand Sport Electric) performance sub-brand".
- s45 (New Corsa GSE, 2026-08-05): same, "GSE" throughout. Derived forms observed:
  "GSE badging", "GSE bumper", "GSE performance seats", "GSE yellow", "GSE logo",
  "GSE suspension bushings".
- Press index (s41) headline slugs are lower-cased by the CMS and headlines are all-caps,
  so neither is evidence either way. The two press-information documents are.

**Likely explanation, offered as a hypothesis and not as evidence:** Opel, the sister
marque, is commonly styled "GSe" in German-market material. Opel's own usage was NOT
probed in this run (www.opel.com is walled, s4). So the safe statement is: *Vauxhall UK
first-party 2026 usage is `GSE`; whether Opel writes it differently is unverified here.*
A guideline should not assume the two marques agree.

## What GSE is, in the brand's own words

- Expansion: **"Grand Sport Electric"** (s53).
- Introduced **2023** as a "performance sub-brand" (s53).
- Positioning phrases, quoted: **"Everyday rally feeling"**, **"From rally to road"** (s53).
- Claimed lineage: GSE models "build on the legacy of GTE and GSi models from recent
  decades" (s53). The release then narrates that heritage with dated specifics: Astra
  GTE Mk1 1984 (1.8-litre, 115bhp, 0-60mph 8.5 seconds), Astra GTE Mk2 (0.30cd, 1988
  2-litre 16-valve "red top", 158hp/145lb/ft), Nova GTE 1987 (100bhp 1.6-litre, 876kg,
  117mph), Corsa GSi 1993 (105bhp at 6,000rpm, 9.2 seconds, 121mph), Astra Mk4 GSi 2002
  (197bhp turbo 2.0-litre, 150mph, 0-62mph 7 seconds).
- Electrification framing: "Now GSE is going fully electric, with the Mokka GSE the
  first of this new era" (s53).
- Motorsport tie: entry in the "ADAC GSE Rally Cup" with the "Mokka GSE Rally", driver
  Ioan Lloyd; the release claims knowledge "gained from the racetrack helps shape
  Vauxhall's cars on the road" (s53).
- Concept car in the lineage: "Corsa GSE Vision Gran Turismo (2025)", also written
  "Corsa GSE Vision GT" (s45, s53), with two 350kW (476hp) motors, 588kW (800hp),
  800Nm, 0-62mph 2.0s, 199mph, 1,170kg.

## VXR: second disagreement with the brief

The brief frames the guideline question as "GSe versus the historic VXR". The evidence
collected says the brand itself does not draw that contrast at all:

- **VXR is never mentioned** in either GSE press-information document (s53 explicitly
  checked for VXR and OPC: "No mention of VXR or OPC anywhere"). The lineage the brand
  claims runs GTE and GSi, skipping VXR entirely.
- s45 does list "Corsa VXR" among heritage names, so VXR is not forbidden, it is simply
  not the reference point for GSE.
- Wikipedia (s43, dated 2025-10-27) states the VXR badge signalled General Motors global
  engineering plus input from Lotus and Triple Eight Racing, and that after PSA acquired
  the marque in 2017 the badge was "ushered into hiatus". Named VXR vehicles: Corsa VXR,
  Astra VXR, Vectra VXR, Insignia VXR, VXR8, Meriva VXR, Zafira VXR, VX220, Monaro VXR,
  VXR8 Maloo. Wikipedia also positions VXR as comparable to Opel's OPC and Holden's HSV.

So: **VXR is a dormant heritage badge (hiatus dated to the 2017 PSA acquisition, per a
third-party source), GSE is the live one (dated 2023, per first-party), and the brand's
own copy connects GSE backwards to GTE/GSi rather than to VXR.** The VXR-hiatus claim
rests on Wikipedia alone and is A3, so medium confidence.
