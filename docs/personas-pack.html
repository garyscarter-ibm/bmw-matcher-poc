The journey is online-first; the purchase stays local. People do the work in the evenings on their own sofa, then buy from a dealer they can reach, inside a radius of roughly forty to fifty miles. The matcher is built for exactly that shape: it is a per-retailer tool, each deployment anchored to a single retailer's stock and honest about what sits just beyond it. This pack covers two live deployments, Grassick's of Perth for BMW and Sytner Luton for MINI. The BMW personas shop the first one's radius, the MINI personas the second's; nobody shops both.

Affordability is the mood. Most buyers budget in monthly payments rather than totals, and younger buyers barely think in any other unit. Taste decides late: people narrow to a shortlist on facts, then choose between near-identical cars on colour and character.

The brands attract different people. BMW's approved-used buyer is established, 35–55, and buys certainty: warranty, history, no surprises. Approved used is also the brand's front door for the next generation, which is what Tyler is here to represent. MINI's buyer skews younger, urban and image-led; the Countryman exists so young families don't have to trade the statement away.

Evidence honesty: every source behind these numbers was fetched and audited on 22 July 2026; each carries the confidence score shown, and two travel-distance statistics from an earlier draft were withdrawn when their claimed source turned out not to contain them. The full audit table, with what each source was used for and why it earned its score, is in docs/personas.md. These personas are synthesised from that research plus brand positioning and this project's stock analysis; they are hypotheses to test, not interview findings.

DO
Daniel Okafor, 47
The Motorway Manager
BMW

“I've had company cars for ten years. This one's my own money, so it needs to be right.”

Life
Regional operations director, outside Perth. Married, two teenagers.
Miles
~22,000 a year, mostly A9/M90
Budget
£22–38k, and he thinks in monthly outgoings too
Shopping for
Diesel or plug-in hybrid saloon/estate
Trigger
Company car goes back on a fixed date in November, found or not
Money
Cash allowance plus savings; no part exchange (the old car is the fleet's)
Device
Desktop, evenings, spreadsheet in the next tab

Says he wants

A comfortable, frugal motorway car. Diesel by habit; hybrid-curious but no home charger yet.

Actually decides on

Total cost of ownership and the absence of doubt. First private purchase in a decade, which makes him the classic Approved Used customer: he is buying the warranty and the history as much as the car. He will read every line of both.

What winning him over looks like

A sensible high-mileage answer with the economy argued in his terms: what this car costs to run at his mileage, and the programme's certainty stated where he can see it rather than assumed.

Checked against live stock, 22 July 2026: a 91% match, 520d M Sport Touring, led with the economy reasoned at his mileage; the plug-in he is curious about was visible as runner-up. Served.

The Family Consolidator
BMW

“If the buggy and the dog don't both fit, it doesn't matter how clever it is.”

Life
GP in Perthshire. Two children under 8, one spaniel. Going from two cars to one.
Miles
~9,000 a year + Glasgow grandparent runs
Budget
£25–45k · wallbox already on the drive
Shopping for
Electric or plug-in SUV/estate
Trigger
The second car failed its MOT in June; the household goes to one good car
Money
Surviving car in part exchange; the rest on a PCP she budgets monthly
Device
Mobile in stolen moments; decides on desktop with partner

Says she wants

An electric family car big enough for everything, safe, and honest about range.

Actually decides on

Whether she believes the boot claim (she thinks in buggies and dogs, not litres), ISOFIX in the back, what the range really is on a cold motorway run to Glasgow, and, buying used electric, what the battery has left: its state of health and whether its warranty carries over.

What winning her over looks like

A shortlist she can defend to her partner: practicality reasons front and centre, honest numbers, and nothing that smells like it is hiding a compromise.

Checked against live stock, 22 July 2026: a seven-way tie at 95%, iX2s and X1 plug-ins all meeting the brief. Served, with friction: near-identical cards, and a stronger match 23 miles away the page never mentioned.

The First-Premium Striver
BMW

“My mates rate the badge. My bank app rates the monthlies. Both have to say yes.”

Life
Software sales, Dundee. Commission just starting to land. First premium car.
Miles
~12,000 a year, commute plus weekends
Budget
£16–24k on paper; really a monthly number and an insurance quote
Shopping for
Petrol 1 Series or 2 Series Gran Coupé, M Sport, black or white
Trigger
First full commission quarter banked in July; the Fiesta's MOT is due by Christmas
Money
~£2k deposit, a part exchange worth barely £1k, ~£300 a month before insurance. Insurance is the ambush
Device
Phone only, late night, YouTube and TikTok reviews running

Says he wants

The M Sport look for the money he has. He knows the exact trims and the exact engines; the reviewers he trusts have opinions on both.

Actually decides on

The monthly payment his bank app tolerates and the insurance quote it doesn't. Then the colour in his building's car park. Approved used is his way into the badge, even if nobody markets it to him that way.

What winning him over looks like

The right car with numbers he can say out loud: the monthly, the insurance group, the 0–62. Zero friction; one whiff of brochure-speak and he's gone.

Checked against live stock, 22 July 2026: an 85% match M135i in Alpine White, his exact car in his colour. Served on the match; his sharpest asks (the monthly, the insurance group) are still on the tool's gap list.

“I don't want clever. I want honest, comfortable, and a plug.”

Life
Recently retired teacher, St Albans. Downsizing from an X1 that's too big for her life now.
Miles
Short: town, garden centre, grandchildren

Plain numbers, no cleverness, and the comfort equipment stated where she can see it.

RJ
Rob Jennings, 41
The Whole-Market Shopper · the one who leaves
REJECTOR

“Why would I take a recommendation from the bloke selling the car?”

Life
Project manager outside Perth. Three tabs open; the retailer's own site holds the fewest cars.
Miles
~14,000 a year, family duty plus commute
Budget
Under £25k, judged against the whole market
Shopping for
3 Series Touring, petrol or diesel; not fussy about the badge if the numbers are better elsewhere
Trigger
None he'd name. Permanently half in the market; a price-drop alert converts him
Money
Part exchange plus a modest deposit; dealer finance only if it beats his bank loan, and he will check
Device
Desktop and phone; saved searches and alerts on everything

Says he wants

A tidy 3 Series Touring under £25k. He knows what the same car costs forty miles away and what the aggregators call a good price.

Actually decides on

Price against the whole market. A single retailer's stock is a sample, not a market, and he treats it that way.

Why he leaves

The moment the tool recommends rather than filters. A matcher operated by the seller is a sales instrument, and he assumes it ranks on margin, not on fit. He is not wrong to assume that.

What would keep him

The tool saying "we do not have the right car for you this week" when that is true. A tool that will admit a miss earns the right to be believed on a hit. Martin gets a version of this as tone; Rob needs it as a system property.

Not yet replayed against stock. His answer set is in the fixtures; his verdict turns on whether the tool will admit a miss, which two of its five result states exist to do.
